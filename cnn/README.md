- What is your track, and what does it look like? Name the modality, the image size and channels, the classes and their balance, and the majority-class baseline. One sentence on what you noticed in the sample grid. 
  - The track we chose was blood. All the images look like purple colored circles in the middle of a brown background. The modality is microscopy on H&E-stained blood cells. The images are 28 by 28 with 3 color channels, and the cells shown were basophils, eosinophils, erythroblasts, immature granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes, neutrophils, and platelets, with the majority cell being neutrophils at 19.5%. The overall spread was rather even.
  - The cells shown on the grid were of different rotations and orientations, and they were not perfectly centered, being off by a few pixels in x and y.

- Is the convolution earning its keep? Give the CNN, the matched-parameter dense net, and the permuted CNN. Interpret the three together: what does the permutation result tell you that the dense comparison alone does not? 
  - The convolution does benefit the model; we see that the CNN accuracy is 0.9003, which is 0.1210 higher than the dense network with matched parameters (accuracy of 0.7793). By looking at the permuted CNN, we can see that it is actually using spatial information because the permutation degrades the performance by a small but significant amount, reducing the accuracy by 0.0757 to 0.8246. Interestingly, the permuted CNN is still better than the matched-parameters dense network. 

- Which invariances did you build in, and which did you deliberately leave out? List the transforms in your augmenter and name one you considered and rejected, with the reason. This is a question about your imaging modality — answer it as one. 
  - We are using the blood cell track. Because there is no inherent orientation in the microscope and the cells themselves are symmetric, rotation and reflection are suitable. While the cells are supposed to be centered, we notice that they are not always perfectly centered and so small translations fall within the spread of the existing data as well. Because the blood cells are essentially identical with translation, rotation, and reflection, these are the transformations we chose in our augmenter. We opted not to use shear or color changing, because the identity of the cell relies heavily on its shape and different color stains.

- What did augmentation buy on the ordinary test set, and on the harder evaluation? Give all four numbers. If the gain on the test set was small and the gain on the harder evaluation was large, say what that means about what a test set measures. 
  - On the original test, CNN without augmentation gives an accuracy of 0.9003, while the CNN with augmentation gives an accuracy of 0.8936, which is a relatively minimal 0.0067 reduction in accuracy. On the harder evaluation, the CNN without augmentation gives an accuracy of 0.8802, while the CNN with augmentation continues to yield a similar 0.8942 accuracy. On the harder evaluation test set, the augmentation gains 0.0140 accuracy over the CNN without it. Likewise, the performance does not drop and in fact very slightly improves between the ordinary test set and the harder evaluation test set, while the CNN without augmentation sees a loss of performance of around 0.0201. 
  - Because the blood test set is relatively flipping/rotation-invariant, the “harder” evaluation set is not actually substantively different from the original data. As a result, the original model continues to perform very similarly with only a minor degradation, while the augmented model performs equally well on both. This does improve the genericity of the model, since it performs better on a harder test set it has never seen before.

- Where do the errors go? Use the confusion matrix and the per-class recalls. Which pair of classes does the model confuse, and does that confusion make sense given what the images look like? 
  - The model confused monocytes and immature granulocytes, and basophils and immature granulocytes, in both cases classifying monocytes and and basophils as granulocytes more frequently than the reverse. This is reasonable given that all of these cells take up most of the field of view and have a dark colored center with a thin light colored boundary.

- What would you do next, with a week and the same data? One concrete thing and what you expect it to change. "A deeper network" is not an answer unless you say what you think is currently limiting the model.
  - Given more time and with the same data, one change we could make is improving the translation-type augmentation. Right now, we use the torch.roll function, but because the background is other non-stained cells, this results in discontinuities being introduced in the rolling direction, which are spurious features. Rather than using the full image with the roll function applied, we could additionally crop all the images by two pixels to eliminate these incorrect features at the edge. If we were doing this approach, we would likely need to crop all the images and have larger held-back images from which we would derive our augmented training data. Further, this improvement would likely not have a major impact because CNNs are relatively translation-invariant in general.
