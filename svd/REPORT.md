1. What is one row of your matrix, and what are you predicting? Two sentences. Name the clinical or physiological decision your label stands in for.
   - Each row of X represents one 28×28 chest X-ray image flattened into 784 pixel values. We predict whether the image is labeled "normal" or "pneumonia," which represents whether the patient who corresponds to this x-ray image has pneumonia and whether they should be referred for further treatment. 
2. What do the first three components look like? Caption each from Figure 2. If a component is brightness, position, or some other nuisance, say so — that is a finding, not a failure.
   - Component 1 represents the overall average brightness, as all the values are positive. By contrast, the intensity of Component 2 is centered around 0, with an even spread of positive and negative values of a similar magnitude. It also appears primarily primarily anatomical, reflecting the outline of lungs and denser tissue. Because the signs are opposite between the lungs and the rest of the tissue, Component 2 likely represents the contrast between the lungs and the rest of the body, which is why it raises accuracy so much, as the tissue contrast is one of the main features that human radiologists look for. Component 3 has high values within the lung, medium values between the lungs, and low values outside of the body. Component 3 is also more asymmetrical than components 1 and 2, which could possibly reflect some pneumonia features.

4. How does your result compare to all three baselines? Give the four numbers. If the no-SVD baseline ties you, say what the decomposition bought you instead.
   - The three baselines of majority class, shuffled labels (control), and raw features no SVD were 0.625, 0.515, and 0.761 respectively. Our result of nearest centroid with k=2 was 0.750, not much worse than using all raw features (at 0.761) and being extremely economical.  
5. Where could this pipeline have cheated, and how do you know it did not? Name the specific line where mu and Vt were fitted, and say which rows they saw.
   - The pipeline could leak information if the mean or SVD components were fitted using test images. In Step 2, `mu = np.mean(X[train_idx], axis=0)` computes the mean from training rows only, and `np.linalg.svd(Xc, full_matrices=False)` fits the components to `Xc = X[train_idx] - mu`. Both therefore use only training rows 0–4707. Test images are projected using the same training-derived mean and components.
6. What would you need to trust this clinically? One paragraph. More subjects? Better labels? A different target? Be concrete.
   - To trust these clinically, we would want to compare our classification method to an existing gold standard, such as that of clinical radiologists. We would also want to ensure that our dataset is representative of diverse groups and diverse imaging systems, or else ensure that it is only used on individuals and systems that match our training dataset. We would also want to periodically validate its performance over time to ensure that if the population changes, the model doesn't lose performance. The last point would be to use a larger dataset and perhaps track changes in performance with training at increasing dataset sizes, to try to determine whether we've reached a plateau in diminishing returns for throwing in more data.
